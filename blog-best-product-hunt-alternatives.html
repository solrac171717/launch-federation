11 Best Product Hunt Alternatives in 2026 (Ranked for SaaS Founders)

Product Hunt still sends real traffic, but a single 24-hour sprint, a nofollow backlink, and a leaderboard that rewards whoever already has the biggest following make it a poor first choice for most early-stage SaaS founders. Below are 11 Product Hunt alternatives actually worth your time in 2026 — ranked by backlink value, launch format, and whether a product with zero existing audience can realistically win a spot.

What makes a good Product Hunt alternative

Not every "launch site" solves the same problem Product Hunt does. Before ranking anything, it's worth being specific about what you're actually optimizing for, because the right alternative depends on it:

  • Backlink quality. Is the link back to your site dofollow or nofollow? A nofollow link does essentially nothing for organic rankings — it's traffic only, not SEO equity.
  • Listing permanence. Does your page stay live forever at its own URL, or does it get buried and effectively disappear once the launch day ends?
  • Fair access. Can a product with zero existing followers actually win a spot, or is visibility reserved for whoever pays, or whoever already has an audience to mobilize votes?
  • Review burden. Is there a real moderation step, or does anyone's listing go live instantly regardless of quality — which quietly devalues every backlink on the site?
  • Community, not just a list. Do real people browse, vote, and comment, or is it a static directory nobody actually visits?

Keep those five in mind as you read the list — they're also exactly what the comparison table below is scored on.

The 11 alternatives, ranked

1. Launch Federation

A weekly directory where launches run Monday-to-Monday instead of fighting for a single 24-hour spotlight. The core difference is the backlink: free listings get a real dofollow link back to your site, and it's badge-verified — we automatically recheck that the badge is still installed every week, so it's not a one-time trick that quietly rots into a dead link six months later. A fixed, small number of free slots opens each week, so a brand-new product with no audience is competing in a fair, bounded pool instead of an infinite paid queue. Premium listings ($7, one-time) skip the free-slot cap entirely and can pick any week, with no badge requirement. If sustained SEO value matters more to you than a single day of vanity traffic, submit your launch here.

2. Product Hunt

Still the biggest audience on this list by far, and still worth using — just not as your only move. The link is nofollow, the leaderboard format means most products are buried by lunchtime, and winning the daily top spot increasingly depends on how many people you can personally mobilize to vote in the first few hours. Good for a traffic spike and social proof; not something to rely on for search rankings.

3. BetaList

One of the oldest startup-discovery lists on the web, built around pre-launch products collecting early signups rather than products that are already live and selling. If you're validating an idea before writing code, BetaList's audience is genuinely useful. Once you have paying customers, it stops being the right fit — the review queue is slow and the format assumes you're still pre-launch.

4. Uneed

A fast-growing, founder-run directory with daily launches and a genuinely active community of builders upvoting and commenting on each other's tools. The link is nofollow and the format is the same daily-leaderboard crunch as Product Hunt, but the community itself is smaller and friendlier, which makes it easier for a brand-new account to actually get seen. Good as a second stop in the same week as Product Hunt, not a replacement for a dofollow-focused directory.

5. Fazier

Lower competition than Product Hunt with a straightforward free tier, plus paid options for guaranteed placement and a dofollow link if you upgrade. Worth it as a low-effort third or fourth submission in a multi-directory launch week — just don't expect the free tier alone to move your SEO.

6. Twelve Tools

Built specifically for small, scrappy tools rather than funded startups with full marketing teams — a good fit if what you shipped is closer to a single-purpose utility than a platform. Listings are permanent, the link is dofollow, and the submission process takes a couple of minutes.

7. Dofollow.Tools

Does exactly what the name promises: a directory built around giving builders a genuine dofollow backlink, with no daily leaderboard format to compete inside. It's one of the more explicitly SEO-minded options here, which makes it a natural companion submission alongside Launch Federation in the same week.

8. KittyLaunch

A newer launch platform with a clean submission flow and its own badge-verification system, conceptually similar to how we handle it here. Worth adding to the rotation if you're already comfortable maintaining one verification badge on your site — adding a second one is low marginal effort.

9. Huzzler

A community-first product directory where other builders can actually try your tool and leave feedback, not just a vote button. The link isn't dofollow, but the qualitative feedback loop is genuinely useful for an early product that still needs user interviews more than it needs backlinks.

10. Peerlist

Originally a professional network for builders and engineers, Peerlist has grown a project-launch feature that doubles as a lightweight Product Hunt alternative inside an already-engaged tech audience. Not a primary SEO play, but a solid place to get your first wave of technical users.

11. Turbo0

A simple, no-friction tool directory — submission takes under a minute and listings go live without a lengthy review queue. The tradeoff is exactly what you'd expect: a nofollow link and a directory that leans more toward raw volume of listings than curation.

When Product Hunt is still worth it

Don't read this as "skip Product Hunt." It's still the single highest-traffic launch day you can get, and a strong showing there gets screenshotted into pitch decks and investor updates for a reason. It's worth it when you already have an audience willing to show up and vote in the first few hours, when you want the social proof badge more than the SEO value, or when you're launching a major version bump that deserves a one-time spike. Just don't treat it as your SEO strategy — pair it with at least one dofollow-focused directory from this list so the launch leaves something permanent behind.

How to launch on several directories without wasting a week

Submitting to ten directories one at a time, each with its own screenshots, tagline, and description box, is how founders burn an entire afternoon on launch logistics instead of talking to users. A tighter process:

  1. Write one canonical description, tagline, and 3–5 screenshots once — reuse them everywhere instead of rewriting copy per site.
  2. Pick one daily-leaderboard directory (Product Hunt or Uneed) and launch it on a single day to consolidate your available votes and attention instead of splitting them.
  3. Submit to your dofollow anchors — Launch Federation and Dofollow.Tools — in the same week, but let them run on their own schedule rather than stacking them against your leaderboard day.
  4. Install the verification badge once, on your homepage footer, and keep it there — it covers every badge-based directory at once instead of maintaining several separately.
  5. Set a recurring monthly reminder to check that every badge and backlink is still live — directories that silently drop broken links won't tell you when yours breaks.

Which alternative fits your stage

Rather than picking randomly off the list above, match the directory to where your product actually is right now:

  • Pre-launch, collecting signups: BetaList first, then come back to the rest once you're live.
  • Just shipped, zero backlinks yet: Launch Federation and Dofollow.Tools, so the very first links pointing at your domain are dofollow instead of nofollow.
  • Have a few hundred users, want a traffic spike: Product Hunt and Uneed on the same day, timed around a real feature launch rather than a routine update.
  • Simple utility, not a full platform: Twelve Tools and Turbo0, where the audience expects smaller, single-purpose tools rather than funded startups.
  • Need qualitative feedback more than traffic: Huzzler and Peerlist, where other builders will actually use the product and tell you what's broken.

Most SaaS founders end up using three or four of these on rotation rather than committing to just one — the directories above solve different problems, not the same one with different branding.

FAQ

What is the best Product Hunt alternative for SaaS founders?

It depends on your goal. For a permanent, SEO-valuable dofollow backlink, Launch Federation and Dofollow.Tools are built specifically for that. For raw launch-day traffic and a large existing audience, Product Hunt itself and Uneed still win on volume.

Are Product Hunt alternatives worth it if I already launched on Product Hunt?

Yes. Most directories accept a product that already launched elsewhere, and submitting to several in the same week compounds backlinks, referral traffic, and discovery without competing against the same audience twice.

Do Product Hunt alternatives give a dofollow backlink?

Most don't by default — Product Hunt's own listing links are nofollow. A handful of alternatives, including Launch Federation and Dofollow.Tools, are built specifically to provide a dofollow link, often tied to keeping a verification badge installed on your site.

How many launch directories should I submit to?

Four to six in the same week is a reasonable range: one weekly, permanent-listing directory as your SEO anchor, two or three daily-launch directories for traffic, and one or two niche or community directories relevant to your product category.

Is it better to launch on multiple directories at once or spread them out?

Launch daily-competition directories (Product Hunt, Uneed, Fazier) on the same day to consolidate the attention spike, but stagger permanent, weekly-cohort directories like Launch Federation across different weeks so each listing gets its own dedicated spotlight instead of competing with your other launches.
