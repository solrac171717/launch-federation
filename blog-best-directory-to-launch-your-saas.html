Why this matters for SaaS SEO specifically

SaaS products compete for keywords against companies with full-time content teams and years of accumulated backlinks. A brand-new domain has none of that history, which is exactly why early, legitimate backlinks from launch directories matter disproportionately in the first six to twelve months — they're often the fastest realistic way to get Google to notice your domain exists at all, long before content marketing has had time to compound. The catch is that most directories don't actually pass any of that value through, because the link is nofollow by default. That's not a minor technicality: a nofollow link tells search engines explicitly not to count it as a vote of confidence, so a founder who submits to thirty directories without checking this gets thirty lines of referral traffic in an analytics dashboard and zero movement in search rankings. The criteria below exist to separate the handful of directories that genuinely help your domain's authority from the much longer list that only helps your traffic for a day.

The 5 criteria that actually matter

Before picking a directory, score it against these five things. Most founders only ever check the first one — traffic — which is exactly why so many launches generate a one-day spike and nothing else.

1. Does the backlink actually count?

Check whether the link back to your site is dofollow or nofollow before you submit anything. Most directories default to nofollow, which does nothing for your search rankings no matter how much traffic the listing sends — Google simply doesn't pass any ranking signal through it. On Launch Federation, free listings get a dofollow link as long as the verification badge stays installed on your site; we recheck it weekly, so it's not something you can install once, remove, and still benefit from. If a directory never mentions dofollow anywhere in its docs, assume it's nofollow and plan accordingly.

2. Is the listing permanent, or does it quietly disappear?

A lot of "launch" sites bury your listing the moment the day ends — technically still online, but with zero internal links pointing to it and zero chance a visitor finds it browsing the site. That's the difference between a one-day traffic spike and a link that keeps compounding in search results for years. Ask directly: does the listing keep its own permanent URL that stays reachable from the site's normal navigation, or does it fall off a leaderboard into the void?

3. Can you actually win, or is it pay-to-win?

Some directories only meaningfully surface listings that paid for placement, which makes a free submission functionally invisible regardless of how good the product is. Others cap free slots at a fixed number per week — creating real competition and real upvotes — while letting paid listings skip the cap and pick any week, so free founders aren't competing against an unlimited queue of paid ones for the exact same spots. Read the fine print on "free tier" before assuming it means visibility.

4. Is there a real audience, or just a database?

A directory with voting, comments, and a leaderboard gives you actual product feedback and discovery from real humans who showed up that day. A directory that's really just a static list — alphabetical, uncategorized, nobody browsing it for discovery — gives you a link and nothing else. Both have a place in your launch plan, but only one replaces doing user interviews.

5. What does it cost to skip the queue?

If you need to launch on a specific date — a funding announcement, a major version bump, a seasonal campaign — check whether the directory has a paid option that bypasses the wait. On Launch Federation, a one-time $7 premium listing bypasses the free-slot cap and lets you choose any week, with no badge requirement. Knowing this number ahead of time means you're not scrambling to find an alternative the week you actually need to launch.

The SaaS launch directory tier list

Instead of ranking every directory on one scale — which pretends they're all trying to do the same job — group them by what they're actually for. Here's how we'd bucket the landscape in 2026:

Tier 1 — Weekly cohort, dofollow-first

Launch Federation sits here: a new weekly batch of launches, a permanent page per product, and a real dofollow link gated behind keeping a verification badge installed. Built for founders who want the backlink to still be doing something for their SEO a year from now, not just the week of launch.

Tier 2 — Daily leaderboard, traffic-first

Product Hunt, Uneed, and Fazier live here. Huge potential reach in a single day, nofollow links by default, and a format that rewards whoever can mobilize the most votes fastest. Use these for the spike, not the SEO. See our full breakdown of Product Hunt alternatives if this tier is your main focus.

Tier 3 — Pre-launch and waitlist

BetaList is the clearest example — built for products collecting early signups before they're fully live. Submit here before you ship, not after; the audience and review queue are both tuned for "coming soon," not "live and selling."

Tier 4 — SEO-first, rolling submission

Dofollow.Tools and Twelve Tools fall here: no daily competition, rolling submissions, and a link built explicitly around SEO value rather than a traffic event. Low effort, permanent upside — these should be on every founder's list regardless of what else you're doing that week.

Tier 5 — Community and feedback

Huzzler and Peerlist are strongest here — other builders actually try the product and leave feedback, which matters more than a backlink when you're still iterating on core features.

Step-by-step: launch across directories in one week

  1. Monday: finalize one canonical tagline, description, and 3–5 screenshots. Write them once, reuse everywhere — this is the single biggest time sink if you skip it.
  2. Monday: install the verification badge on your site's footer if you're going the free route on any badge-based directory. Do this before submitting anything, not after.
  3. Tuesday: submit to your Tier 1 and Tier 4 picks (Launch Federation, Dofollow.Tools, Twelve Tools) — these run on their own schedule and don't need to be timed against anything else.
  4. Wednesday–Thursday: prepare your Tier 2 submission (Product Hunt or Uneed) — line up a handful of people who'll genuinely use the product and ask them to check it out and vote when it's live, rather than cold-asking strangers.
  5. The following Tuesday: launch your Tier 2 pick on a weekday, early in your target audience's morning — avoid Mondays (crowded) and weekends (lower traffic).
  6. Same week: post to one Tier 5 community (Huzzler or Peerlist) and actually read every comment — this is where you'll hear the feedback that matters most at this stage.
  7. One month later: check that every badge and every backlink is still live. A broken badge silently flips your link to nofollow on badge-based directories, and nothing will notify you when it happens.

Mistakes founders make when submitting

  • Launching everything on the same day. Daily-leaderboard directories should be grouped together; permanent, weekly-cohort directories should be spread out so each listing gets its own spotlight instead of competing with your other launches for the same attention.
  • Never checking dofollow vs. nofollow. A founder who submits to twenty directories without checking this ends up with twenty links that do nothing for search rankings, and finds out only when a backlink audit tool points it out months later.
  • Treating a badge as a one-time install. If a badge-based backlink gets accidentally removed in a site redesign, the dofollow link quietly reverts to nofollow — and most directories, including ours, make this an automatic, recurring check for exactly that reason.
  • Reusing a pre-launch pitch after shipping. Submitting "coming soon" copy to a directory's live audience months after launch reads as abandoned or stalled — update the copy to reflect that the product is actually live and has users.
  • Ignoring the comments. The vote count is the vanity metric; the comments are where you find out what to build next. Skipping them wastes the one part of a launch that's actually free market research.

FAQ

What's the best directory to launch a SaaS product?

There isn't a single best one — it depends on whether you need a permanent SEO backlink, a one-day traffic spike, or early user feedback. A weekly, permanent-listing directory with a dofollow link (like Launch Federation) works best as your long-term anchor, paired with one or two daily-launch directories for an initial spike.

How long does a SaaS launch directory listing stay live?

It varies by directory. Daily-leaderboard sites technically keep your page online forever but bury it after the launch day ends, so it stops getting organic traffic. Permanent, cohort-based directories keep your listing discoverable on its own page indefinitely.

Do I need to pay to launch my SaaS on a directory?

No. Most reputable directories, including Launch Federation, offer a free tier. Paid tiers typically buy you a guaranteed placement, no badge requirement, or the ability to skip a queue — useful if you need to launch on a specific date, not required otherwise.

What is a launch directory tier list based on?

A useful tier list groups directories by what they're actually good for — permanent SEO backlinks, one-day traffic spikes, pre-launch waitlists, or community feedback — rather than ranking them all on a single scale, since they solve different problems.

What's the biggest mistake founders make when submitting to launch directories?

Treating every directory the same and submitting to all of them on the same day. Daily-leaderboard directories should be launched together to consolidate attention; permanent, weekly-cohort directories should be spread across different weeks so each listing gets its own dedicated spotlight.
